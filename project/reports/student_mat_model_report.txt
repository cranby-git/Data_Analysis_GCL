1. Executive Summary

This report evaluates the factors influencing student performance in secondary mathematics education and details the performance of a machine learning model developed directly in BigQuery ML. Using demographic, social, and prior academic data from 395 students, a linear regression model was trained to predict the final exam score (G3, scale 0–20).

The resulting model demonstrates high predictive capability:

    Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$): 0.8220, accounting for 82.2% of the variance in final mathematics grades.
    Mean Absolute Error (MAE): 1.29 points (out of 20), with 50% of predictions within 0.81 points of actual grades.
    Convergence: The training converged smoothly across 17 iterations, reducing loss from 18.26 to 3.73.

The model provides an effective foundation for early-warning academic risk monitoring, allowing educators and administrators to flag at-risk students well before the final examination.

4. Limitations

    Information Leakage via Intermediate Grades (G1, G2):
        Incorporating G1 and G2 alongside non-academic features substantially inflates model accuracy because prior test performance is heavily correlated with the final outcome.
        If predictions are required at the beginning of the semester before G1 or G2 are recorded, this model cannot be applied in its current structure.
    Small Dataset Sample Size ($N = 395$):
        The sample size is limited, and 88.4% of the population originates from a single school (GP), reducing generalizability to other school systems or educational regions.
    Evaluation on Full Dataset / Overfitting Risk:
        The reported evaluation was run against the training data without an explicit out-of-time or held-out test split, meaning test metrics may mildly overstate real-world performance on unseen cohorts.
    Zero-Inflation in Final Scores:
        Several students score 0 in G3 (often caused by dropouts, disciplinary disqualification, or absence from the final exam). Linear regression models struggle with structural zeroes, predicting negative or low fractional scores instead.

    Build a Two-Stage Early-Warning Pipeline:
        Stage 1 (Pre-Semester Baseline): Train a model excluding G1 and G2 to identify baseline risk factors (e.g., prior failures, study habits, absences) before classes begin.
        Stage 2 (In-Semester Updated Forecast): Use the current model incorporating G1 and G2 midway through the term to flag students tracking toward failure.

Long-Term Enhancements

    Explore Non-Linear & Tree-Based Models:
        Benchmark the linear model against BOOSTED_TREE_REGRESSOR or RANDOM_FOREST_REGRESSOR in BigQuery ML to capture interactions between social habits (e.g., alcohol consumption combined with high absences).
    Address Zero Scores (Two-Step Hurdle Model):
        Model student retention/exam attendance first using a logistic classification model (LOGISTIC_REG to predict whether G3 > 0), followed by regression for students taking the exam.
    Operationalize via Looker / Dashboards:
        Expose model predictions using ML.PREDICT into a Looker Studio dashboard for academic counselors to facilitate targeted interventions (e.g., assigning supplementary tutoring before G3 exams).
