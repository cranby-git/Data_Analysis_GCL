1. Executive Summary

This report evaluates the ingestion, statistical distribution, and predictive modeling of Bitcoin daily market data over a 7-day period (August 1, 2017 – August 7, 2017). Using BigQuery ML, a linear regression model was trained to predict the daily closing price (Close) based on intraday market metrics: opening price (Open), daily high (High), daily low (Low), trading volume (Volume), and market capitalization (Market Cap).

The resulting model demonstrated strong statistical convergence and high apparent explanatory power on the training sample, yielding an $R^2$ of 0.9622, a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of $41.69, and a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of $50.14 relative to an average closing price of $2,996.48. However, due to severe sample size constraints ($N = 7$), high feature-to-sample ratio, and structural target leakage from contemporaneous metrics (particularly Market Cap, High, and Low), these metrics represent an in-sample fit rather than a robust forecasting tool. Strategic recommendations are provided to build a production-grade predictive pipeline.
2. Methodology
2.1 Data Ingestion & Schema Definition

The dataset was ingested from bitcoin/bitcoin_price_week1.csv into Google Cloud BigQuery under the table identifier: project-842dbf51-43bc-4833-997.agent_dest_dataset.bitcoin_price_week1.

The table schema consists of:

    Date (STRING): Date of observation (Aug 01, 2017 – Aug 07, 2017).
    Open, High, Low (FLOAT): Daily intraday price levels in USD.
    Close (FLOAT): Final daily settlement price in USD (Regression Target).
    Volume (INTEGER): Total aggregate daily trading volume.
    Market Cap (INTEGER): Total circulating market valuation in USD.

2.2 Model Architecture & Training Configuration

    Algorithm: Ordinary Least Squares Linear Regression (LINEAR_REG).
    Target: Close.
    Predictor Features: Open, High, Low, Volume, Market_Cap.
    Optimization & Training: Executed over 20 iterations (steps 0 to 19) via BigQuery ML. Learning rates dynamically adapted between 0.20 and 0.80, reducing mean training loss from 5,758,314.39 down to 2,513.99 with an average execution duration of ~944 ms per step.

4. Limitations

    Extreme Sample Size Constraint ($N = 7$): A sample of seven data points is statistically insufficient for generalizable machine learning. With 5 predictor variables and 1 intercept term, the model operates with only 1 degree of freedom ($df = N - p - 1 = 7 - 5 - 1 = 1$). This almost guarantees overfitting and inflated $R^2$ values.
    Lookahead Bias & Data Leakage: Features such as High, Low, Volume, and Market Cap are end-of-day summary figures realized concurrently with Close. In a real-world trading setting, these values are unavailable ahead of time to predict the day's close.
    Severe Multicollinearity: Market Cap is defined as $\text{Price} \times \text{Circulating Supply}$. Consequently, Market Cap is nearly a linear transformation of the target variable Close. Combined with Open, High, and Low, the predictors exhibit extreme collinearity, which inflates parameter variance and destabilizes coefficient reliability.
    Temporal Non-Stationarity: Cryptocurrency time series exhibit non-stationary regimes, volatility clustering, and macroeconomic regime shifts. Training on a single bullish week from 2017 prevents the model from handling bearish, sideways, or higher-volatility environments.

5. Recommendations
5.1 Data Pipeline & Expansion

    Expand Historical Scope: Ingest multi-year historical data across varying market cycles (bull, bear, consolidation) to scale the dataset from 7 rows to several thousand rows.
    Standardize Schema Typing: Convert the Date column from STRING to BigQuery native DATE or TIMESTAMP to support automated time-series windowing and partitioning.

5.2 Feature Engineering for True Predictive Utility

    Lagged Feature Generation: Replace same-day contemporaneous features with historical lag variables (e.g., $Close_{t-1}$, $Volume_{t-1}$, 7-day rolling moving averages, Exponential Moving Averages, RSI, and MACD) to eliminate lookahead bias.
    Remove Direct Leakage: Exclude Market Cap or compute percentage change metrics ($\Delta \text{Market Cap}_{t-1}$) rather than absolute levels.
    Predict Log Returns: Rather than predicting raw nominal price levels, model daily log returns ($\ln(Close_t / Close_{t-1})$) to ensure stationarity.

5.3 Advanced Modeling Techniques

    Time-Series Split / Cross-Validation: Replace random shuffling or in-sample evaluation with chronological forward-chaining splits (e.g., train on Days $1..T$, test on Day $T+1$).
    Adopt Native Time-Series Models: Transition from basic linear regression to models designed for sequential financial data, such as BigQuery ML's ARIMA_PLUS, or tree-based regressors (BOOSTED_TREE_REGRESSOR) with explicit lag features.
