1. Executive Summary

This report evaluates the ingestion, exploratory data analysis, and predictive modeling performed on daily Bitcoin trading data covering the week of August 1, 2017 to August 7, 2017. A Linear Regression model (LINEAR_REG) was trained directly within Google Cloud BigQuery ML to predict Bitcoin's daily Opening Price (Open) using intraday price extremes, closing prices, volume, and market capitalization.

The model achieved an apparent $R^2$ of 0.9622 and a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of $50.14 (relative to an average open price of $2,926.13). However, the training dataset consists of only 7 rows, and features such as same-day High, Low, and Close introduce severe multicollinearity and lookahead bias. While mathematically converged, the model is not suitable for production trading or directional forecasting without fundamental structural adjustments.
2. Methodology

    Data Ingestion:
        The source dataset bitcoin/bitcoin_price_week1.csv was ingested into Google BigQuery under project-842dbf51-43bc-4833-997.agent_dest_dataset.bitcoin_price_week1.
        The data was parsed into standard numeric types (FLOAT for prices, INTEGER for volumes and market capitalization, STRING for dates).

    Target & Feature Definition:
        Target Variable: Open (continuous float value representing daily open price in USD).
        Candidate Predictors: High, Low, Close, Volume, and Market Cap.

    Model Selection & Architecture:
        Given the continuous target, BigQuery ML’s LINEAR_REG (Linear Regression) algorithm was implemented.
        The model was trained using BigQuery ML’s native gradient-based optimization over 20 iterations with adaptive learning rates ranging between 0.20 and 0.80.

    Evaluation Framework:
        Evaluation was conducted using ML.EVALUATE, tracking Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Median Absolute Error, Mean Squared Log Error (MSLE), and Explained Variance ($R^2$).

4. Limitations

    Extreme Sample Size Constraint ($N = 7$):
        Training a 5-feature linear regression model on only 7 data points results in just 1 degree of freedom ($N - k - 1 = 7 - 5 - 1 = 1$).
        The high $R^2$ value (0.9622) is primarily a mathematical artifact of parameter saturation, creating severe overfitting rather than genuine generalizability.

    Data Leakage & Lookahead Bias:
        In financial markets, an opening price occurs at the beginning of the session ($t=0$).
        Using same-day features like High, Low, and Close implies having end-of-day knowledge to predict the start-of-day price, which is impossible in real-time execution.

    Collinearity:
        High, Low, Close, and Market Cap are intrinsically collinear in asset pricing. This inflates coefficient variance and degrades predictive stability.

    Narrow Temporal Window:
        A single week from August 2017 cannot capture market cycles, structural regime shifts, macro volatility, or modern cryptocurrency market structure.

5. Recommendations

    Restructure as a Proper Lagged Time-Series:
        Shift features so the model uses historical values (e.g., $Close_{t-1}$, $High_{t-1}$, $Low_{t-1}$, $Volume_{t-1}$) to predict the next session’s $Open_t$.
        Remove contemporaneous daily features to eliminate lookahead leakage.

    Expand Historical Depth:
        Ingest at least 2–3 continuous years of daily historical data (700+ rows) or switch to hourly granularity to provide adequate statistical power.

    Adopt Time-Series Specific Algorithms:
        Leverage BigQuery ML's native univariate/multivariate time-series models such as ARIMA_PLUS or BOOSTED_TREE_REGRESSOR with lag windows.

    Implement Temporal Validation:
        Avoid standard random train/test splits. Utilize a walk-forward, rolling-window validation split (e.g., train on Days $1 \dots T$, test on Day $T+1$) to accurately measure real-world predictive utility.
